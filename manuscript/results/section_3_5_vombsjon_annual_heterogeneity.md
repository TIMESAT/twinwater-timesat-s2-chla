### 3.5 Annual heterogeneity of the locked Vombsjön transfer

The aggregate transfer summaries concealed substantial annual variation in reconstruction performance. Figure 5 resolves this variation for primary ACOLITE MCI under four-acquisition withholding, the largest withholding block evaluated. Whereas Figure 4 summarizes equal-year behavior across withholding designs, Figure 5 shows how pointwise error, withheld-date trajectory correlation and conditional observed-proxy peak timing differed among the ten years from 2017 to 2026.

The annual pointwise results showed both consistent and variable method patterns (Figure 5a). Within the primary ACOLITE evaluation, linear interpolation had lower annual nRMSE than TIMESAT smoothing spline in every Vombsjön year and withholding design. Its comparison with default TIMESAT double logistic was less uniform: under isolated withholding, each method had lower annual nRMSE in five years, whereas linear interpolation had lower annual nRMSE in eight of ten years under four-acquisition withholding. The two exceptions under four-acquisition withholding were 2018 and 2020. In 2020, default TIMESAT double logistic had annual nRMSE of 0.225, compared with 0.276 for linear interpolation, reversing their aggregate ordering.

Annual withheld-date trajectory correlation also varied across years and did not reproduce the pointwise ordering consistently (Figure 5b). In 2020, correlation was 0.735 for default TIMESAT double logistic and 0.691 for linear interpolation, accompanying the lower double-logistic nRMSE in that year. In 2026, however, TIMESAT smoothing spline had the highest trajectory correlation among the three methods despite having higher nRMSE than linear interpolation. Thus, the annual comparison depended on whether agreement was evaluated through normalized pointwise error or correspondence among withheld-date values.

Conditional observed-proxy peak timing exposed further differences among years and methods (Figure 5c). Each annual mean absolute peak error summarized four reference-eligible peak scenarios per method-year. These scenarios were nested within the same year and did not represent four independent seasons. The reference remained the available quality-controlled observed-MCI maximum within annual support, rather than the true ecological bloom peak. In 2020, default TIMESAT double logistic had a mean absolute peak error of 5.25 days, compared with 42 days for linear interpolation. The same year therefore combined lower double-logistic nRMSE with substantially smaller conditional peak error, demonstrating that the annual departure from the aggregate pointwise ordering extended to another reconstruction property.

The partial 2026 support remained included in the annual and equal-year summaries. ACOLITE observed support extended only through 3 August 2026. Under four-acquisition withholding, annual nRMSE was 0.367 for linear interpolation, 0.433 for default TIMESAT double logistic and 0.421 for TIMESAT smoothing spline, while mean absolute observed-proxy peak errors were approximately 95–107 days across the three methods. These large timing errors describe reconstruction relative to the maximum within the available observations; that maximum is not interpreted as a full-calendar-year ecological maximum.

The 2018 results were also retained as part of the withheld-satellite-proxy evaluation. No valid contemporaneous fixed-polygon field pairs were available in 2018, so its transfer results do not constitute contemporaneous field validation.

Annual variation therefore materially qualified the aggregate transfer pattern. Lower equal-year pointwise error did not imply the same method ordering in every year, and annual nRMSE, trajectory agreement and conditional peak timing remained distinct assessments of reliability. Retaining all eligible years, including the difficult and partial-support cases, made these differences visible.
