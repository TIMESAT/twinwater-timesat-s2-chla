### 3.2 Metric-specific reconstruction under the Erken actual observation mask

Reconstruction of the dense Erken reference under the actual observation mask showed different performance patterns for pointwise accuracy, global-peak timing and common-support seasonal integral (Figure 2). All three methods produced reconstructions with available metrics in each of the seven years, 2019–2025. Nevertheless, complete reconstruction output did not imply consistent preservation of the evaluated seasonal properties. Annual estimates and equal-year summaries revealed both differences among methods and substantial variation in peak-timing performance.

Linear interpolation had the lowest descriptive equal-year nRMSE, at 0.203, followed by TIMESAT smoothing spline at 0.223 and default TIMESAT double logistic at 0.250 (Figure 2a). The corresponding 95% whole-year cluster-bootstrap intervals were 0.154–0.250, 0.186–0.257 and 0.216–0.286. The annual results supported a consistent pointwise pattern: linear interpolation had lower nRMSE than default double logistic in all seven years and lower nRMSE than the smoothing spline in six of seven years. The spline had slightly lower nRMSE than linear interpolation in 2019. Linear interpolation therefore had the lowest pointwise error among the three evaluated methods under the actual observation mask.

Global-peak timing was substantially more year-sensitive than the pointwise nRMSE pattern (Figure 2b). Linear interpolation placed the reconstructed common-support maximum within ±10 days of the reference maximum in five of seven years; default double logistic and the smoothing spline each met this criterion in four years. Large timing errors were concentrated in 2020 and 2025. In 2020, absolute errors were 31 days for linear interpolation and 151 days for both default double logistic and the smoothing spline. In 2025, the corresponding errors were 200, 207 and 202 days. These annual extremes contributed strongly to equal-year mean absolute peak errors of 34.6, 54.4 and 54.6 days, respectively. The lower equal-year mean for linear interpolation therefore coexisted with substantial timing errors and did not establish stable timing superiority. These peak metrics refer to the global maximum within each year’s common support; because 2019 and 2025 were boundary-truncated, their common-support maxima are not interpreted as full-calendar-year ecological maxima.

The method pattern differed for the common-support seasonal integral (Figure 2c). Default double logistic had the lowest descriptive equal-year mean absolute integral error, at 126.0 µg·d/L, compared with 157.5 µg·d/L for linear interpolation and 187.1 µg·d/L for the smoothing spline. Existing paired bootstrap intervals for the double-logistic integral advantage included zero, so this lower mean does not establish statistical superiority. The contrast with timing was evident in 2020: default double logistic had an absolute integral error of only 6.2 µg·d/L despite its 151-day global-peak error. Thus, close agreement in the common-support integral could coexist with a large displacement of the reconstructed maximum.

Equal-year normalized absolute peak-magnitude error was 0.797 for linear interpolation, 1.039 for TIMESAT smoothing spline and 1.304 for default TIMESAT double logistic, using the common-support reference Q95−Q05 normalization.

Across the evaluated properties, linear interpolation combined lower pointwise error with lower equal-year normalized peak-magnitude error, whereas default double logistic had lower descriptive integral error. Global-peak timing remained strongly dependent on the year. Pointwise accuracy, seasonal timing, peak magnitude and integral preservation therefore provided distinct assessments of reconstruction reliability under the actual observation mask.
